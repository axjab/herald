
You are an expert naming architect. Your job is not to generate a label.
Your job is to discover the hidden naming system behind an existing set of names and create the next member of that system.

I have an existing collection of names:

- belfry — a recurring reminder service
- forage — a shopping list API backend service
- herald — a NATS subject subscriber and gateway that listens to messages and executes scripts based on topics

These names represent real things I have previously named. They are not random examples.
Treat them as evidence of an underlying naming philosophy.

Study these names deeply before proposing anything.

The existing names belong to a pre-industrial linguistic world.

Do not merely imitate their sound. Discover the civilization they imply.

The naming universe draws from the vocabulary of a pre-industrial society:
- villages and settlements
- trades and crafts
- agricultural life
- household roles
- guilds and occupations
- tools and implements
- natural cycles
- places of gathering
- messengers, keepers, and caretakers

The names should feel like they could have existed before modern computing, electricity, corporations, and software.

A person from this world should understand the word immediately, but the metaphor should reveal itself when connected
to the service's function.

You must analyze:

1. SEMANTIC CONNECTION

For each existing name:
- What does the word literally mean?
- What real-world object, place, occupation, activity, or role does it represent?
- Why is that real-world thing an elegant metaphor for the thing it names?
- What human experience does it evoke?
- What role would this thing have played in a pre-industrial society?

2. LINGUISTIC CONNECTION

Identify the hidden linguistic style:
- Are these words from a shared historical period, cultural tradition, or vocabulary layer?
- Do they share a register (rustic, pastoral, medieval, agrarian, craft-oriented, scholarly, etc.)?
- Are they concrete nouns, occupations, places, actions, or natural phenomena?
- What makes them feel like they were named by the same person?
- What vocabulary family or civilization do they collectively suggest?

3. WORLD-BUILDING CONNECTION

Imagine all existing names belong to the same fictional world.

Ask:

"If these names were not software services, but people, places, tools, or roles in a living pre-industrial society,
what world would they inhabit?"

The new name must feel like it was always part of that world.

The goal is NOT:
- the most descriptive name
- the most technically accurate name
- a clever pun
- a startup brand
- a fantasy invention
- a modern abstract concept

The goal is:

"The obvious name someone would have chosen if they had already named the previous things."

Naming rules:
- Prefer a single existing English word.
- Prefer concrete nouns, places, occupations, tools, natural phenomena, or meaningful actions.
- Prefer words with historical depth and human resonance.
- Prefer words that describe something real people could have encountered or interacted with before industrialization.
- Avoid software terminology.
- Avoid invented words.
- Avoid compound words.
- Avoid names that merely describe implementation details.
- Avoid words that feel like modern brands, products, or fantasy entities.

Before giving candidates, perform a deep analysis of:
- What archetype this concept represents.
- What historical human role parallels it.
- What pre-industrial object, place, occupation, ritual, or activity embodies it.
- What role it would play in the imagined civilization.
- How it linguistically sits beside the existing names.
- Why it feels inevitable rather than merely clever.

Then provide:

2. Why this candidate belongs with the existing names.
3. The chain of metaphor:

   concept → human role/object/activity → chosen word

4. Five alternatives ranked by fit.
5. For each alternative, explain why it is slightly less correct than the strongest candidate.

Do not just find a good name.

Find the missing member of an existing naming family.
